# Urethritis

*Category: Urinary Health*

## Definition

Urethritis is inflammation of the urethra, the tube that carries urine from the bladder out of the body (and, in men, also carries semen). It can be caused by infection or by non-infectious irritation.

## Causes

Urethritis is grouped by cause:

- Gonococcal urethritis: caused by the bacterium Neisseria gonorrhoeae (gonorrhoea).
- Non-gonococcal urethritis (NGU): all other causes. The most important infectious cause is Chlamydia trachomatis. Others include Mycoplasma genitalium, Trichomonas vaginalis, herpes simplex virus and some other viruses and bacteria. In some men no organism is found.
- Non-infectious causes: injury (for example from a catheter or medical instrument), or irritation from soaps, spermicides, lubricants or other chemicals.

## Relationship with infections / STIs

- Gonorrhoea and chlamydia are the two bacterial infections of proven clinical importance in men with urethritis. Both are sexually transmitted.
- Many other causes of urethritis are also passed on through sex, so partners of a person with urethritis usually need to be tested and treated.
- Infections can be present without symptoms, so a person can pass one on without knowing.
- Urethritis caused by an STI can make it easier to catch or pass on HIV.
- Because gonorrhoea and chlamydia are reportable infections and need partner notification, doctors try to identify the exact cause with testing.

## Symptoms

- Burning or pain when passing urine (dysuria)
- Discharge from the penis (clear, white, yellow or green)
- Itching, tingling or irritation at the opening of the penis
- Needing to urinate more often or urgently
- Some people have no symptoms at all (asymptomatic infection is common)

## Risk factors

- Sex without a condom
- A new sexual partner, more than one partner, or a partner with an STI
- A previous STI or previous urethritis
- Being young and sexually active
- Recent catheter use or urethral instrumentation
- Using products that irritate the genital area

## Prevention

- Use condoms consistently and correctly
- Reduce the number of partners, or stay in a mutually monogamous relationship with a tested partner
- Have regular STI screening, especially with new partners
- Make sure recent partners are tested and treated, and avoid sex until treatment is complete
- Avoid soaps and chemicals that irritate the genital area

## Diagnosis / testing

A healthcare professional asks about symptoms and sexual history and examines the genital area. Urethritis is confirmed by finding signs of urethral inflammation, such as discharge, white blood cells in a urethral swab smear, or in a first-void urine sample. Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) on urine or a urethral swab identify gonorrhoea and chlamydia, and testing for other organisms may follow if symptoms persist or return. Testing for other STIs, such as HIV and syphilis, is usually offered too.

## Treatment overview

General information only. Treatment is chosen by a healthcare professional.

- Infectious urethritis is treated with antibiotics chosen for the likely or confirmed cause.
- Sexual partners usually need testing and treatment at the same time to prevent reinfection.
- People are advised to avoid sex until treatment is finished and symptoms have settled.
- Non-infectious urethritis is managed by removing the irritant and easing symptoms.
- If symptoms continue or return, further testing looks for less common causes.

## Complications

- Infection spreading to the epididymis (epididymitis) or the prostate
- Narrowing of the urethra (stricture) after repeated or severe inflammation
- Reactive arthritis after some infections
- Passing the infection to partners, who may develop serious problems such as pelvic inflammatory disease
- Increased chance of catching or passing on HIV

## When to seek medical help

- Any discharge from the penis or burning when urinating
- Sex without a condom with a new partner, or a partner diagnosed with an STI
- Symptoms that do not improve after treatment or that return
- Pain or swelling in a testicle

## Urgent warning signs (extra, for the website page)

- Fever with severe testicle pain or swelling
- Being unable to pass urine
- Severe pelvic or lower abdominal pain

## Myths vs Facts

| Myth | Fact |
| --- | --- |
| Urethritis is always caused by an STI. | Infection is the most common cause and many infections are sexually transmitted, but injury and chemical irritants can also cause it. |
| If there is no discharge, there is no infection. | Asymptomatic infections are common, so a person can have and pass on an infection without symptoms. |
| Urethritis and a urinary tract infection (UTI) are the same thing. | Both can cause burning when urinating, but urethritis affects the urethra and is often sexually transmitted. The tests and treatment differ. |
| Leftover antibiotics or home remedies can clear it. | The right medicine depends on the cause, so testing comes first. Wrong treatment can leave the infection in place. |
| Once treated, you cannot get it again. | Reinfection is possible, especially if a partner has not been treated. |

## Key Facts

- Urethritis is inflammation of the urethra, most often caused by infection.
- Gonorrhoea and chlamydia are the main bacterial causes of urethritis in men.
- Many people with urethritis-causing infections have no symptoms.
- Sexual partners should be tested and treated to stop reinfection.
- Condoms and regular STI screening lower the risk.
